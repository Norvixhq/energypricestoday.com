Oil jumps over 4% as Netanyahu and Trump remarks stoke worries of prolonged Middle East conflict

Staff May 11, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil rallied sharply Wednesday as geopolitical tensions in the Middle East intensified following recent statements from Israeli and U.S. leadership. The broader energy complex reacted to renewed concerns that regional instability could disrupt supply flows through critical chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz. Traders reassessed their risk premium, pushing both WTI and Brent higher amid uncertainty over the scope and duration of potential escalation.

Comments from Netanyahu and Trump suggesting a hardened stance on Middle East policy renewed fears of prolonged conflict that could strain global energy supplies. A significant portion of the world’s oil passes through the Persian Gulf, making any credible threat to shipping lanes or production infrastructure a material driver for crude prices. Market participants historically view geopolitical risk as a floor under oil valuations when military or political action appears more likely.

Energy analysts have long monitored OPEC+ responses to regional instability, given the bloc’s concentration of spare capacity in the Gulf. Any disruption to major producers such as Saudi Arabia or Iran would immediately tighten global markets and support higher prices. The speed and magnitude of today’s move reflect how quickly sentiment can shift when escalation risks emerge.

Investors will likely remain focused on official statements from major oil producers and U.S. policymakers in coming sessions. Traders are also watching crude inventories, rig activity, and any signals about OPEC+ production decisions, which could either amplify or temper the current risk-driven rally. The durability of this move depends heavily on whether geopolitical risks are seen as temporary or structural to the near-term outlook.
