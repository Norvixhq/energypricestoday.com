Oil Price Today (May 13): Crude oil snaps 3-day fall ahead of Trump’s China visit. What are experts saying

Staff May 14, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil trading halted a three-session decline on May 13, with market participants attributing the rebound to anticipation surrounding the incoming U.S.–China diplomatic engagement. The upcoming visit represents a potential shift in trade dynamics, which has weighed on energy demand forecasts and investor sentiment in recent weeks. Traders are reassessing near-term supply and demand scenarios as geopolitical and trade developments evolve.

Energy analysts have underscored the sensitivity of crude markets to U.S.–China relations, given the implications for global economic growth and petroleum consumption. Negotiations around tariffs, trade barriers, and industrial policy carry downstream effects on refining margins, transportation, and energy-intensive manufacturing. Market observers are watching for any signals that could clarify the trajectory of energy demand in one of the world’s largest consuming regions.

The technical bounce follows weakness earlier in the week, though broader market fundamentals remain subject to conventional supply-and-demand pressures. OPEC+ production decisions, global inventory levels, and activity in major oil-producing regions continue to anchor price moves alongside geopolitical risk premiums. Traders are also monitoring macro indicators and currency movements, which historically influence commodity valuations.

Experts recommend tracking incoming economic data and official statements from both governments to gauge the durability of any near-term price recovery. The outcome of high-level discussions could reset market expectations for energy demand and prompt reassessment of existing positions in futures and derivatives markets. Market participants should remain alert to both upside and downside catalysts as diplomatic talks progress.
