Oil Price Today (May 13): Crude oil snaps 3-day fall ahead of Trump’s China visit. What are experts saying

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil reversed a three-session decline on May 13, with traders reassessing risk ahead of high-level U.S.-China diplomatic talks. The bounce reflected cautious optimism that trade negotiations could ease tensions that have weighed on global energy demand. Geopolitical developments and policy signals typically drive short-term volatility in crude markets, and this week’s political calendar is no exception.

Energy analysts remain divided on the broader implications of U.S.-China engagement for oil markets. Some observers see potential for demand recovery if trade friction moderates, while others warn that structural economic headwinds could persist regardless of diplomatic progress. The outcome of high-level talks will likely shape crude’s trajectory over the next few weeks, though fundamental supply-demand balances will ultimately dominate longer-term price direction.

OPEC+ production decisions and global inventory levels continue to underpin the market’s baseline. Any meaningful shift in geopolitical risk premium would need to be weighed against current market fundamentals, including refinery utilization rates and seasonal demand patterns. Market participants are monitoring both crude futures and spreads for signals about physical supply tightness.

Traders are advised to watch for official statements and readouts from trade negotiations, as well as key economic data that could influence near-term sentiment. The energy market’s sensitivity to policy outcomes means volatility may persist until clearer signals emerge about the trajectory of U.S.-China relations. Investors should remain focused on both headline risk and underlying supply-demand dynamics.
