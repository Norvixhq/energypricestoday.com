Oil prices stable as Trump insists ceasefire still intact after U.S.-Iran exchange fire

Staff May 8, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks held their ground Wednesday as geopolitical tensions in the Middle East remained elevated but contained. The U.S. and Iran exchanged military fire in recent days, yet messaging from the Trump administration has sought to frame the incident as isolated rather than a broader escalation. Market participants are parsing official statements carefully, as any widening conflict in the region could disrupt global oil supply.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne oil passes, remains a critical focal point for energy traders. While the recent exchange did not directly threaten shipping lanes, the proximity of hostile actions to one of the world’s most important chokepoints underscores underlying risks. Refiners and hedgers continue to monitor developments closely, though current price stability suggests markets are taking the ceasefire claims at face value.

Broader oil market fundamentals have provided some ballast amid geopolitical noise. Global supply remains relatively stable, with OPEC+ production management and U.S. shale output continuing to influence pricing dynamics. Demand concerns from slower economic growth in some regions have also tempered upside pressure that Middle East tensions might otherwise create.

Energy market participants will watch for any further escalation or official statements that could alter the current risk assessment. The durability of the stated ceasefire and diplomatic channels will be key to whether crude volatility picks up in coming days. For now, the market’s measured response reflects cautious optimism that the recent military exchange remains an outlier rather than the start of a broader conflict.
