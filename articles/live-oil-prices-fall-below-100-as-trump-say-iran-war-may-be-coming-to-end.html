Live: Oil Prices Fall Below $100 as Trump Say Iran War May Be Coming To End

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil benchmarks retreated from three-figure territory on Monday following remarks from U.S. President Donald Trump suggesting a potential de-escalation in the Iran conflict. West Texas Intermediate and Brent crude both pulled back as markets reassessed geopolitical risk premiums that had supported prices in recent weeks. The shift reflects investor sentiment that a reduction in regional tension could ease supply concerns that have underpinned the energy complex.

Iran’s nuclear program and regional proxy activities have long served as a flashpoint for oil market volatility, given the nation’s role as a significant crude producer and its control of chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz. Any credible signal of diplomatic progress or military de-escalation typically prompts traders to lower the geopolitical risk premium embedded in prices. The current pullback suggests markets are pricing in a lower probability of supply disruptions tied to Iranian assets or energy infrastructure.

Energy traders will remain focused on OPEC+ production decisions and the broader macroeconomic backdrop, which continue to shape price direction independent of geopolitical headlines. Demand concerns, U.S. dollar strength, and inventory levels remain key drivers alongside any fresh developments in U.S.–Iran relations. The market’s reaction underscores how sensitive crude is to both supply-side geopolitical risk and demand-side economic signals.

Investors should monitor official statements from U.S. and Iranian officials for clarity on the trajectory of tensions, as well as any changes to sanctions regimes that could affect Iranian crude exports. Further price moves will depend on whether rhetoric translates into concrete policy shifts or whether the market views current statements as preliminary positioning. Energy markets will price in additional supply if a genuine reduction in conflict risk materializes.
