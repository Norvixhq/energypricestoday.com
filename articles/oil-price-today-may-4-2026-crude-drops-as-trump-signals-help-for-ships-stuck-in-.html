Oil price today (May 4, 2026): Crude drops as Trump signals help for ships stuck in Strait of Hormuz; sta

Staff May 5, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks retreated on Sunday following signals from the Trump administration that it would take action to clear shipping congestion in the Strait of Hormuz. The indication eased near-term supply concerns that had supported prices earlier in the week. Brent and WTI both moved lower as investors reassessed geopolitical risk premiums baked into recent trading.

The Strait of Hormuz remains one of the world’s most critical energy chokepoints, with roughly one-fifth of global oil passing through its waters daily. Vessel delays and operational disruptions in the region can tighten supply and push prices higher within days. Any credible commitment to restore normal traffic flow addresses a genuine market constraint.

Trump’s signaled intervention reflects broader pressure to stabilize energy costs ahead of the summer driving season and midterm economic data releases. Market participants are weighing whether direct U.S. involvement could meaningfully reduce bottlenecks or merely provide temporary relief. The outcome will likely depend on the scope and timeline of any coordinated effort.

Energy traders are monitoring official statements and shipping updates closely for details on implementation. A sustained resolution of Hormuz congestion could prove supportive for downstream refining margins and consumer fuel prices. However, uncertainty remains on how long any relief measures would persist and whether underlying tensions in the region persist.
