Oil prices flat as Trump’s plan for Strait of Hormuz fails to calm market

Staff May 5, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks remained range-bound Tuesday as markets absorbed the Trump administration’s latest position on securing the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint through which roughly one-third of global seaborne oil passes daily. The lack of price movement suggests traders are unconvinced the plan will materially reduce geopolitical risk in one of the world’s most strategically critical waterways. Both WTI and Brent traded near recent midpoints, with neither bulls nor bears gaining clear momentum.

The Strait of Hormuz has long been a flashpoint for crude volatility, particularly given regional tensions involving Iran and maritime chokepoint control. Previous announcements regarding security measures have often failed to shift sentiment if market participants doubted their effectiveness or durability. The administration’s current approach appears to fall into that category, with traders waiting for concrete evidence of implementation before repricing risk premiums.

Supply fundamentals elsewhere continue to anchor the broader price picture. OPEC+ production levels, refinery utilization rates, and global demand signals remain the primary drivers of crude direction, suggesting that geopolitical measures alone carry limited sway absent a material shift in physical supply-demand balance.

Energy markets will likely remain sensitive to any escalation in regional tensions or disruptions to transit routes, but Tuesday’s muted response indicates skepticism about the near-term impact of the Strait security plan. Traders will continue monitoring implementation details and any signals of actual risk reduction before committing directional bets on the outcome.
