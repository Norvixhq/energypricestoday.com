Oil prices today: Crude rises over 1% as Donald Trump warns Iran, Hormuz concerns persist

Staff May 16, 2026 5 min read

Global crude benchmarks gained ground on Tuesday, with geopolitical tensions in the Middle East supporting sentiment among traders. Fresh rhetoric from incoming U.S. leadership toward Iran has reignited concerns over regional stability and potential disruptions to one of the world’s most critical oil transport routes. The combination of supply-side anxiety and technical buying pushed both WTI and Brent into positive territory for the session.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a focal point for market participants, given that roughly one-third of seaborne traded crude passes through the waterway annually. Any escalation in U.S.–Iran tensions historically translates to risk premiums in energy prices, as investors price in the possibility of shipping delays or supply disruptions. Current geopolitical messaging has kept traders cautious about downside momentum in the near term.

Market analysts note that crude’s modest gains reflect a balance between bullish geopolitical headlines and underlying concerns about global demand. Winter demand patterns in the Northern Hemisphere typically support prices, though macroeconomic uncertainties continue to weigh on longer-term outlooks. Traders remain closely attuned to any further policy shifts that could reshape regional dynamics.

Going forward, the energy market will likely respond to both official statements from Washington and any tactical developments along shipping lanes in the Persian Gulf. OPEC+ supply management decisions and production levels in key non-member nations will also remain critical drivers of price direction. Investors should monitor official announcements and geopolitical wires for additional clarity on the trajectory of tensions.
