Why are oil prices up today, and will Brent and US WTI crude futures rise sharply or fall again? Global oi

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Crude futures advanced today amid a combination of supply-side concerns and demand signals from major consuming regions. OPEC+ production decisions, geopolitical tensions, and seasonal refinery maintenance all contributed to upward pressure on both Brent and WTI contracts. Market participants are closely monitoring whether these moves reflect structural support or a temporary bounce.

On the supply side, ongoing output constraints in key producing nations and shipping risks through critical chokepoints have underpinned sentiment. Conversely, mixed signals from global economic data—particularly manufacturing and fuel demand indicators—keep traders cautious about sustainability of any rally. The typical late-summer seasonality, when refineries reduce runs, also influences the near-term price trajectory.

Brent and WTI spreads remain volatile, with transatlantic differentials reflecting logistics and regional inventory levels. Any sharp upside move now faces headwinds from emerging recession concerns and potential demand destruction if energy prices persist at elevated levels. Technical support and resistance levels are being tested as traders weigh fundamental data against macro uncertainty.

Investors should expect continued volatility until clearer signals emerge on OPEC+ policy and global growth. The direction of crude futures will largely depend on whether supply tightness persists or demand weakens faster than production adjustments can offset. A watchlist of refineryutilization rates, rig counts, and weekly inventory data will be essential for tracking the next significant price move.
