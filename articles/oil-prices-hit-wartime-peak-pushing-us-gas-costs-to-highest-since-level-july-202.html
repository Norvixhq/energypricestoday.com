Oil prices hit wartime peak, pushing U.S. gas costs to highest since level July 2022

Staff May 2, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil benchmarks have reached levels unseen since the early days of Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, reflecting renewed supply concerns and geopolitical tension in key producing regions. The surge has rippled through global energy markets, with refiners passing costs along the supply chain. WTI and Brent crude, the world’s primary pricing benchmarks, both reflect the sharp upward pressure.

U.S. retail gasoline prices have climbed in tandem, reaching their highest point since July 2022 as crude costs translate directly to the pump. Regional variation remains significant, with Gulf Coast refineries and markets dependent on waterborne imports facing steeper pressure than inland areas. The national average now reflects both crude strength and seasonal refining dynamics heading into peak driving months.

Supply tightness in the Atlantic Basin and Middle East shipping lanes continues to underpin prices, while OPEC+ production levels remain a focal point for traders. Any disruption to flows through the Strait of Hormuz or unplanned maintenance at major facilities would likely intensify upward pressure. Market participants are closely monitoring whether current geopolitical risks persist or stabilize.

Energy consumers and policy makers are watching for signs of demand destruction or strategic reserve releases that could ease price pressure. Refiners operating at elevated margins are incentivized to maximize throughput, though crude availability and logistics constraints may limit their response. The trajectory of prices in coming weeks will depend heavily on whether supply concerns prove temporary or sustained.
