Oil prices drop and stock markets rise after reports of deal to end Iran war

Staff May 12, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil retreated across both benchmarks on Tuesday following reports that diplomatic negotiations have produced a framework to resolve the conflict between Iran and regional adversaries. The potential de-escalation reduces immediate supply-side risks that have kept traders on edge for months. Risk assets, including equities, rallied in tandem as geopolitical premium dissipated from energy markets.

A reduction in Middle East tensions typically lowers the probability of port closures, shipping disruptions, or direct infrastructure damage in one of the world’s most critical oil-producing regions. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne oil passes, would face diminished risk of blockade or attack. Traders have priced in less insurance for supply shocks, allowing WTI and Brent to settle lower on the day.

Energy markets remain sensitive to any headline suggesting escalation or de-escalation in Iran-related conflicts, given the region’s outsized influence on global crude balances. While prices have declined, underlying fundamentals—including OPEC+ production decisions and global demand signals—continue to shape the medium-term outlook. The reported deal does not immediately alter inventory levels or refinery utilization rates.

Market participants will monitor official statements from involved governments and any formal agreement details in coming days to confirm the durability of this diplomatic progress. If sustained, reduced geopolitical risk could allow oil prices to trade more closely to supply-demand technicals rather than conflict premiums. For now, the mood shift has rewarded safe-haven equities and penalized crude as near-term hedging demand eases.
