Oil and gas prices fall sharply, driven by hopes of strait of Hormuz reopening – as it happened

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

Crude futures retreated across both benchmarks on Wednesday as market sentiment shifted toward diplomatic resolution in the Middle East. The prospect of renewed shipping traffic through one of the world’s most critical chokepoints eased concerns about supply disruption that had supported prices in recent sessions. Traders weighed the geopolitical risk premium against the reality of potential crude flow normalization.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne oil passes annually, has long served as a barometer for regional tensions and energy security. Any credible signal of de-escalation tends to reduce the scarcity premium embedded in contract prices, as market participants reassess inventory needs and production forecasts. Wednesday’s moves reflected that classic risk-off dynamic in commodities tied to geopolitical flashpoints.

Both WTI and Brent crude declined as investors rotated out of safe-haven energy positions. The shift underscored how swiftly sentiment can reverse when headline risk moderates, particularly in a market already watching for demand weakness and OPEC+ production decisions. Refined products tracked crude lower, though trading volumes remained elevated ahead of potential policy developments.

Energy markets will remain sensitive to any updates on strait negotiations and broader regional stability in coming sessions. Traders and refiners are adjusting operational hedges in real time as probabilities shift between closure and reopening scenarios. The underlying fundamentals of supply, demand, and inventory will ultimately determine whether the recent price relief proves durable or temporary.
