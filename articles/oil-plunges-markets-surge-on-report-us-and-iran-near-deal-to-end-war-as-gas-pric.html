Oil plunges, markets surge on report U.S. and Iran near deal to end war as gas prices jump past $4.50

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil futures fell sharply Tuesday following reports that U.S. and Iranian negotiators have made progress toward ending regional hostilities. The potential de-escalation eased concerns about supply disruptions in the Middle East, a region responsible for roughly one-third of global oil production. Equity markets rallied on the news, with investors rotating away from safe-haven assets and into risk-on positioning.

Despite the crude decline, gasoline prices at the pump have climbed past $4.50 per gallon in many U.S. regions, reflecting a lag between wholesale energy markets and retail fuel pricing. Refining margins remain elevated, and local distribution costs continue to support elevated pump prices even as crude futures retreat. Consumers may see further softening at the pump only if crude prices stabilize at lower levels and the wholesale-to-retail spread normalizes.

The geopolitical shift reduces immediate risk premiums that had been priced into WTI and Brent crude following months of regional tensions. A sustained de-escalation would remove uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20 percent of globally traded oil flows. However, energy markets remain sensitive to any sign that negotiations could falter or that other supply disruptions may emerge.

Traders are now watching for formal confirmation of any agreement and monitoring OPEC+ production decisions, which could influence crude direction in coming weeks. The divergence between falling crude prices and sticky retail gasoline suggests the supply chain may be adjusting unevenly as sentiment shifts. Energy investors should track both diplomatic developments and fundamental market data before positioning for the next phase of volatility.
