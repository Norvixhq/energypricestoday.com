Oil Prices Fall Below $100 After Trump Pauses Hormuz Escort Plan

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil benchmarks declined in early trading after reports that the U.S. administration is stepping back from plans to escort tankers through the Strait of Hormuz. The shift in maritime security policy reduced immediate concerns about potential supply disruptions in one of the world’s most critical chokepoints. West Texas Intermediate and Brent futures both moved lower on the geopolitical relief.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a flashpoint for energy markets, with roughly one-third of seaborne oil passing through its narrow corridor daily. Any credible threat to transit — whether from regional tensions, mine-laying, or naval incidents — historically triggers sharp price spikes. A pause on U.S. escort operations signals reduced expectation of imminent confrontation in the region.

Market participants had priced in elevated risk premiums ahead of the escort initiative announcement. The reversal suggests traders are recalibrating their near-term supply assumptions, with fewer hedges against a Hormuz blockade appearing necessary. Demand-side weakness and ample global inventories are also weighing on sentiment.

Energy markets remain sensitive to geopolitical headlines and any future policy reversals regarding the Gulf region. While this week’s pullback provides a reprieve, long-term structural concerns around Middle Eastern stability and the region’s outsized influence on global oil supply persist. Traders will monitor official statements and regional developments for clues about the durability of this de-escalation.
