Oil Prices Waver as Market Weighs Chances of U.S.-Iran Deal

Staff May 10, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks showed mixed signals Thursday as traders reassessed the likelihood of negotiations between Washington and Tehran producing a near-term accord. The uncertainty reflects a persistent tension in the market: a potential easing of U.S. sanctions on Iranian oil exports could increase global supply, yet any deal remains contingent on diplomacy that has historically proven fragile. Investors are caught between two competing impulses—relief at a possible resolution and caution about premature positioning.

An accord affecting Iranian crude would carry outsized importance for global energy balances, given Tehran’s estimated production capacity and the current tight margins in refining markets. Traders are monitoring official statements from both sides for concrete signals, while acknowledging that political obstacles could derail talks at any stage. The current price range suggests the market has only partially priced in the possibility of a breakthrough.

Broader market fundamentals remain supportive for crude demand, with seasonal consumption patterns and industrial activity in major economies providing underlying support. However, the Iranian wildcard has introduced enough volatility to prevent a sustained directional move in either direction. Investors are largely staying on the sidelines until clarity emerges from the negotiation front.

Energy analysts note that any final agreement would likely include phased sanctions relief, meaning Iranian barrels would not flood markets immediately. That timeline consideration has muted the urgency to either buy or sell based on deal speculation alone. Market participants should expect continued choppiness until negotiators signal material progress or an impasse becomes evident.
