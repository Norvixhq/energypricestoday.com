Oil prices edge lower as U.S. waits for Iran response to deal proposal

Staff May 7, 2026 5 min read

Crude futures declined modestly today as markets absorbed uncertainty around ongoing nuclear negotiations between the United States and Iran. Traders are adopting a cautious stance ahead of Tehran’s formal response to Washington’s latest proposal, with the possibility of a renewed deal keeping some downward pressure on prices. The lack of clarity on timing and terms has left investors hesitant to build fresh long positions.

A successful agreement could eventually ease sanctions on Iranian crude exports, potentially adding barrels to an already-well-supplied global market. This prospect has weighed on sentiment, though the energy sector continues to monitor geopolitical developments closely given Iran’s strategic position near the Strait of Hormuz. Any escalation in tensions would reverse the selling pressure immediately.

U.S. crude inventory data and refinery utilization figures due later this week may provide additional direction for prices in the near term. Domestic production remains stable, and the Energy Information Administration’s weekly reports will offer investors fresh insight into demand fundamentals and commercial stockpile levels.

Markets will likely remain range-bound until Iran signals its negotiating position more explicitly. In the interim, other factors—including OPEC+ compliance with production cuts and seasonal demand trends—will help guide intraday trading. Energy traders are advised to monitor official statements from Tehran and Washington for concrete signals.
