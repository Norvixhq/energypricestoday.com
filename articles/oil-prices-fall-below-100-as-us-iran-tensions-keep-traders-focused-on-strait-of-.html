Oil prices fall below $100 as U.S.-Iran tensions keep traders focused on Strait of Hormuz risks

Staff May 7, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks retreated this week as traders weighed competing pressures on global supply and demand. While geopolitical tensions in the Middle East remain a structural floor under prices, concerns about demand growth and swelling inventories have pushed buyers to take profits. The risk premium embedded in the market reflects legitimate supply-chain vulnerabilities, but isn’t translating into sustained price strength at present.

The Strait of Hormuz remains the focal point for risk assessment among energy traders and analysts. Any disruption to the waterway—through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne oil passes—would constrain supplies and likely reset price expectations higher. Current geopolitical friction keeps traders attentive to headlines, but without an imminent escalation, the market is defaulting to fundamental supply-demand dynamics.

U.S. crude inventories and refinery utilization rates continue to shape near-term direction alongside international developments. Weekly Department of Energy data on petroleum stocks and processing activity provide concrete signals about domestic consumption and storage levels. These flows often outweigh sentiment when prices settle into lower ranges, creating a more data-driven trading environment.

Energy markets will remain sensitive to any diplomatic or military developments affecting Iranian crude exports or regional chokepoints. Until a material shift occurs in either supply availability or demand signals, the price floor will depend on how traders balance upside risks against softer fundamentals. Monitoring official OPEC+ statements and vessel tracking through the Strait will remain standard practice for market participants.
