U.S. crude oil tops $100 again as hope fades for a U.S.-Iran peace deal

Staff May 12, 2026 5 min read

West Texas Intermediate crude broke above the $100 per barrel level on Monday, driven by renewed concerns over Middle Eastern geopolitical tensions and slimming prospects for a diplomatic resolution between the United States and Iran. The rally marks a significant shift from earlier expectations that a potential nuclear agreement could ease sanctions pressure and allow more Iranian oil onto global markets. Traders are pricing in supply risk once again as diplomatic channels appear to narrow.

Tensions over Iran’s nuclear program have long weighed on crude prices, particularly given the country’s strategic position near the Strait of Hormuz — a chokepoint through which roughly 20% of global petroleum flows. Any disruption to shipping through the strait, whether from military action or retaliatory measures, would sharply constrain global oil supplies. Market participants have grown accustomed to using the Iran risk premium as a baseline in their price models.

The latest move higher also reflects broader OPEC+ production management and persistent questions about global crude demand heading into winter. With members focused on supporting prices through coordinated output cuts, additional geopolitical friction in the Persian Gulf provides natural support for valuations above $100. The market is also watching near-term inventory data and refinery utilization rates for clues on demand resilience.

For energy traders and hedgers, the renewed strength above the century mark signals a return to elevated uncertainty premiums that characterized earlier periods of Iran tensions. Whether crude can sustain these levels will depend on both diplomatic developments and incoming macroeconomic data that affect consumption forecasts. For now, the fading peace deal narrative has reversed months of downside pressure on crude values.
