Brent oil pulls back after climbing to $126 per barrel on U.S.-Iran escalation fears

Staff Apr 30, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks retreated from intraday highs on Monday as markets digested the fallout from escalating U.S.–Iran tensions. Brent crude had spiked to $126 per barrel earlier in the session before reversing course, reflecting the typical volatility seen when geopolitical risk premiums spike and then partially unwind. The pullback suggests traders are reassessing the scope and duration of potential supply disruptions in the region.

Geopolitical friction in the Middle East remains a core driver of oil sentiment, particularly given the Strait of Hormuz’s critical role in global energy flows. Any sustained escalation could threaten shipments through one of the world’s most important chokepoints, though markets have also grown accustomed to periodic flare–ups without major infrastructure damage. The balance between risk appetite and risk aversion will likely determine whether crude consolidates near current levels or experiences further volatility.

Energy markets are closely monitoring official statements and any signals from major producers about output decisions or supply security measures. OPEC+ production policy and U.S. inventory data remain key data points that could either amplify or dampen current price movements. A clearer picture of the geopolitical situation’s scope could provide direction for the coming days of trading.

For now, Brent crude’s retreat from its session high underscores the tension between supply concerns and demand uncertainty in a softening global economy. Traders are watching for both headline developments and fundamental energy market indicators to determine whether the recent spike represents a durable shift in pricing or a temporary repricing of risk. The coming week’s data and diplomatic developments will be critical in shaping the direction of crude benchmarks.
