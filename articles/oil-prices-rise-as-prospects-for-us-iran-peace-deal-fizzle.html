Oil Prices Rise as Prospects for U.S.-Iran Peace Deal Fizzle

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks climbed on Friday as diplomatic efforts between Washington and Tehran showed diminishing signs of progress. The deterioration in negotiations removed a key downside risk that had weighed on energy markets for weeks. Traders now see reduced likelihood of near-term sanctions relief that could have unlocked Iranian barrels into global supply.

A potential U.S.–Iran nuclear accord would have eased restrictions on Iranian oil exports, threatening to add as much as 1 million barrels per day to an already-balanced market. The collapse of these talks eliminates that overhang, allowing prices to reflect tighter supply fundamentals. Geopolitical risk premiums have returned to crude’s valuation as the probability of a deal has receded.

Market participants are now reassessing supply dynamics without assuming Iranian crude will flood global markets in the coming months. OPEC+ production targets and compliance remain the primary drivers of price direction in the near term. The focus has shifted back to demand signals and refining margins as traditional market fundamentals regain prominence.

Energy traders will watch for any renewed diplomatic signals, though sentiment has clearly shifted away from optimism about a quick resolution. Until negotiations show concrete signs of revival, the geopolitical friction between the two nations is likely to support crude valuations. The energy market’s repricing underscores how closely linked Middle Eastern politics remain to global oil supply expectations.
