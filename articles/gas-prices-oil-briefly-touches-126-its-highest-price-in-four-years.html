Gas prices: Oil briefly touches $126, its highest price in four years

Staff May 2, 2026 5 min read

West Texas Intermediate crude reached $126 per barrel intraday, marking the highest level since 2020 and signaling sustained pressure on global energy markets. The move reflects a combination of supply concerns and geopolitical tensions that have kept crude elevated for weeks. This level, if sustained, would represent a significant shift from the $80–$90 range that prevailed earlier in the year.

OPEC+ production decisions and the ongoing impact of Western sanctions on Russian oil have tightened the market’s supply outlook. Additionally, refinery maintenance schedules and seasonal demand patterns have contributed to the upward momentum. The intraday touch of $126 underscores how quickly crude can spike when inventory buffers tighten.

At the pump, consumers are already feeling the effects of elevated crude prices, with gasoline and diesel tracking the underlying oil market. Regional variations in fuel supply and local tax structures mean pump prices vary widely, but the general trajectory remains upward. Motorists and logistics operators should expect continued volatility as long as crude remains under pressure.

Market participants are watching for signals from OPEC+ meetings, U.S. crude inventory reports, and any shifts in geopolitical risk that could either support or undercut current price levels. The $126 print demonstrates that even brief touches of elevated prices can materialize when sentiment shifts or supply concerns resurface. Traders remain cautious given the sensitivity of global markets to any disruption in key transit routes or production regions.
