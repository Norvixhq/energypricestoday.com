Gas prices: Oil briefly touches $126, its highest price in four years

Staff May 3, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil reached $126 per barrel on recent trading, marking the highest level since 2020 and signaling renewed pressure on global energy markets. The move reflects tightening supply dynamics and lingering geopolitical concerns that have kept prices elevated throughout the year. WTI and Brent benchmarks both participate in these swings, though regional and refining factors create price differentials between grades.

Supply constraints continue to drive the rally. OPEC+ production decisions, combined with limited spare capacity and ongoing disruptions in certain regions, have limited the market’s ability to absorb demand spikes. Traders are closely monitoring compliance with output agreements and watching for any signals of additional supply management in coming months.

Demand remains a balancing factor as consumers respond to higher prices. Elevated pump prices at the gas station translate into reduced consumer spending on fuel, though industrial and aviation demand have proven relatively resilient. Macroeconomic uncertainty, however, keeps markets vigilant about potential demand destruction if prices remain at these levels.

The $126 level, while notable, reflects broader volatility in energy markets rather than a sustained breakout. Analysts are watching key technical levels and fundamental shifts in supply-demand balance to determine whether recent highs hold or give way to consolidation. Geopolitical headlines and refinery maintenance schedules will remain critical factors for near-term price direction.
