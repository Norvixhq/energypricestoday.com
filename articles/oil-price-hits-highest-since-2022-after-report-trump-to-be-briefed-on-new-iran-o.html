Oil price hits highest since 2022 after report Trump to be briefed on new Iran options

Staff May 3, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil benchmarks rallied to their strongest levels in roughly two years following reports that the incoming administration plans to brief President Trump on additional policy options regarding Iran. The advance reflects market concern that new diplomatic or military strategies could disrupt Iranian crude exports or escalate regional tensions around key shipping lanes. Brent and WTI both benefited from the risk premium typically embedded when geopolitical uncertainty rises in the Middle East.

Iran’s current crude export capacity remains a focal point for global energy markets, with sanctions and operational challenges already constraining production volumes. Any further pressure on Iranian output would tighten supply just as demand estimates remain firm. Market participants are watching closely for details on which options may be under consideration, knowing that the range spans from stricter enforcement of existing sanctions to more aggressive measures.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint for roughly one-fifth of seaborne oil trade, making regional stability central to price formation. Traders have historically priced in elevated risk premiums during periods of elevated U.S.-Iran tensions, particularly when policy shifts signal a harder line. Current price strength suggests the market is already assigning meaningful probability to tighter supply conditions ahead.

Energy analysts note that the timing and scope of any policy shift will likely determine the magnitude of market response. If the briefing results in concrete announcements, volatility could persist as refiners and traders reassess supply availability and hedging strategies. The oil market has priced in some geopolitical risk, but confirmation of specific measures could prompt additional repricing across the crude and refined product complex.
