Oil price hits highest since 2022 after report Trump to be briefed on new Iran options

Staff May 2, 2026 5 min read

Crude markets surged on Monday following reports that the incoming administration is preparing a range of policy options regarding Iran’s nuclear program and regional activities. The move signals a potential shift in U.S. energy diplomacy that could reshape supply dynamics in one of the world’s most geopolitically sensitive regions. Traders interpreted the headlines as a sign of tighter sanctions or confrontation, both historically supportive of oil valuations.

Iran remains a central variable in global oil supply calculations, with the country’s production and export capacity subject to international sanctions regimes. Any escalation in U.S. policy toward Tehran typically constrains Iranian crude flows to international markets, tightening the overall supply picture. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of the world’s seaborne oil passes, also lies within Iran’s sphere of influence, adding a geopolitical risk premium to prices.

West Texas Intermediate and Brent crude both climbed on the news, reflecting investor concern about potential supply disruptions. Energy markets are particularly sensitive to Iran-related headlines given the region’s ongoing tensions and the structural importance of Middle Eastern production to global balances. The rally underscores how policy shifts, rather than immediate demand or supply changes, can drive sharp price movements.

Market participants will likely await further details on the administration’s Iran strategy before adjusting long-term price forecasts. The duration and scope of any new measures will be critical to assessing their impact on crude supply and prices in coming months. Refiners and consumers should monitor official statements for clarity on implementation timelines and potential exemptions that could affect energy costs.
