U.S. crude oil posts biggest one-day drop since 2020 on U.S.-Iran ceasefire agreement

Staff May 4, 2026 5 min read

WTI crude oil experienced its sharpest single-day decline since 2020 following news of a ceasefire agreement between the United States and Iran. The deal reduces immediate geopolitical risk in a region that accounts for a significant share of global crude exports. Market participants rapidly reassessed the probability of supply disruptions tied to Middle Eastern tensions.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne oil transits, has been a focal point of concern given U.S.–Iran tensions. A ceasefire lowers the risk premium that traders had priced into crude contracts. This de-escalation removes one of the key upside catalysts that had supported oil prices in recent weeks.

Energy markets had been sensitive to any escalation in the region, particularly regarding potential threats to tanker traffic and production infrastructure. The agreement suggests a lower probability of tit-for-tat strikes that could disrupt crude flows or refining capacity in the Persian Gulf. Traders moved quickly to exit risk-on positioning built around geopolitical premiums.

The selloff reflects broader market relief but does not necessarily alter fundamental supply–demand dynamics or OPEC+ production decisions. Crude volatility may remain elevated until the terms of any longer-term agreement are clarified and verified. Energy market participants will continue monitoring regional developments and any adjustments to output policy.
