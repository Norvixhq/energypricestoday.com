Oil plunges, markets surge on report U.S. and Iran are near deal to end war as gas prices jump past $4.50

Staff May 7, 2026 5 min read

Reports of diplomatic progress between Washington and Tehran sent crude lower on Tuesday, as investors weighed the prospect of reduced geopolitical risk in the Middle East. WTI and Brent both retreated from recent highs, though the magnitude of declines remained modest given the sensitivity of oil markets to regional tensions. Equities responded more decisively to the headline, with major indices posting gains as risk appetite improved.

The mechanism behind oil’s decline is straightforward: a potential agreement ending hostilities could ease supply concerns tied to the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne crude passes daily. Any de-escalation in the region historically reduces the risk premium baked into crude valuations. However, market participants remain cautious, given the complexity of ongoing negotiations and the history of failed diplomatic efforts in the region.

Gasoline markets moved in the opposite direction, with pump prices climbing past $4.50 per gallon in several states despite crude’s weakness. This divergence reflects seasonal refining dynamics and localized supply constraints rather than crude fundamentals alone. Refiners have faced tighter margins and feedstock challenges in recent weeks, limiting their ability to pass crude savings directly to retail prices.

Energy traders and analysts are monitoring developments closely, aware that any confirmed agreement—or conversely, any breakdown in talks—could trigger sharp reversals across both crude and products markets. For now, the modest crude decline suggests the market is pricing in only a partial reduction in geopolitical risk, leaving room for further volatility if negotiations accelerate or stall.
