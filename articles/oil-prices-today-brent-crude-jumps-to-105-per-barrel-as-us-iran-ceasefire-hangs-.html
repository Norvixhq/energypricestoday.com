Oil prices today: Brent crude jumps to $105 per barrel as US-Iran ceasefire hangs on ‘life support’

Staff May 12, 2026 5 min read

Brent crude surged to $105 per barrel on Monday amid escalating tensions between Washington and Tehran, with diplomatic efforts to restore the 2015 nuclear agreement showing signs of deterioration. Market participants cited heightened geopolitical risk premium as the primary driver, offsetting softer demand signals from China and Europe. The rally underscores oil’s sensitivity to supply disruption fears in the Middle East, where Iran controls one of the world’s largest proven reserves.

Negotiations to revive the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) have stalled in recent weeks, leaving analysts uncertain whether sanctions relief will materialize. Both sides have publicly criticized the other’s negotiating position, and several rounds of indirect talks have yielded no breakthrough. If the ceasefire framework collapses entirely, the risk of military escalation could trigger a more sustained price spike, particularly if Iranian crude exports face further restrictions.

US crude prices followed Brent higher but remained at a discount, reflecting domestic supply dynamics and refinery utilization rates. The weekly EIA petroleum inventory report, due later this week, will provide critical data on crude demand and stockpile trends. Traders are also monitoring OPEC+ compliance with its production quotas ahead of the group’s next scheduled meeting.

Energy analysts remain divided on the sustainability of current price levels, with some viewing the move as temporary geopolitical noise and others warning of deeper supply-side risks if diplomatic channels close completely. Hedging activity and positioning data suggest increased risk-off sentiment in energy markets. Investors should watch official statements from both the US State Department and Iranian officials for any signals of renewed dialogue or hardline rhetoric.
