Oil prices today: Brent crude jumps to $105 per barrel as US-Iran ceasefire hangs on ‘life support’

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Brent crude oil surged to $105 per barrel today, driven by geopolitical risk premiums tied to escalating tensions in the Middle East. The sharp move reflects investor concern that a fragile diplomatic arrangement could collapse, reigniting regional conflict and threatening global oil supplies. Traders are closely monitoring developments from Washington and Tehran for any sign of breakthrough or breakdown in negotiations.

The ceasefire between the US and Iran has faced mounting pressure from both hardliners and structural disagreements over sanctions relief and nuclear compliance. Energy markets have priced in a significant risk buffer, with each negative headline triggering bids for crude as a hedge against potential supply disruption. A full breakdown in talks could push prices even higher, given the strategic importance of the Strait of Hormuz to global petroleum flows.

WTI crude and other benchmarks have followed Brent upward, though at a slightly narrower premium reflecting the domestic US supply picture. Refiners and traders are already modeling contingency scenarios for supply loss, while OPEC+ members are watching to see whether production cuts would be needed to support prices if Iranian exports face new restrictions. The $105 level represents a critical psychological threshold that hasn’t held firmly since mid-2022.

Market participants caution that oil’s reaction remains data–dependent on diplomacy rather than fundamentals. Demand indicators from China and the broader economic outlook still weigh on longer–term sentiment, meaning prices could reverse sharply if negotiations stabilize or if recession fears intensify. The coming days will be critical for determining whether this rally has lasting support or proves temporary.
