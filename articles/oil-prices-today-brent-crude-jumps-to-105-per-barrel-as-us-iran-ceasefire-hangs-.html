Oil prices today: Brent crude jumps to $105 per barrel as US-Iran ceasefire hangs on ‘life support’

Staff May 14, 2026 5 min read

Brent crude surged to $105 per barrel Wednesday as geopolitical tensions in the Middle East weighed heavily on market sentiment. The spike reflects investor anxiety over the fragility of ongoing ceasefire negotiations between the US and Iran, with traders pricing in additional supply risk. WTI futures also climbed in tandem, though regional dynamics and refinery maintenance schedules continue to shape intraday volatility.

Tensions around the Strait of Hormuz have long been a flashpoint for oil markets, given that roughly one-fifth of global crude shipments transit the waterway annually. Any escalation in US-Iran hostilities threatens chokepoint transit and could trigger cascading supply disruptions across Europe, Asia, and North America. Current ceasefire discussions remain precarious, leaving oil traders glued to diplomatic developments.

OPEC+ production decisions and broader macroeconomic headwinds have also kept crude elevated in recent weeks. While the cartel’s output management helps support prices, geopolitical premiums can amplify moves beyond fundamental supply-demand balances. Energy analysts note that a full diplomatic breakdown could push Brent well above current levels, though sustained prices above $105 depend on how ceasefire talks unfold.

Downstream markets and refiners are monitoring crude moves closely, as margin compression and maintenance schedules already weigh on global refining capacity. Shipping costs and insurance premiums for tankers in contested waters may rise further if diplomatic risks escalate. Oil market participants should watch for any official statements from US or Iranian officials in the coming sessions, as such signals often trigger sharp intraday swings.
