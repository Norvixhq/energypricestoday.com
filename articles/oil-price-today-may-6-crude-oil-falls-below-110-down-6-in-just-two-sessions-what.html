Oil Price Today (May 6): Crude oil falls below $110, down 6% in just two sessions. What’s behind the dip?

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil has slipped below the $110 per barrel mark in rapid succession, wiping out roughly 6% of its value over just two trading sessions. The sharp pullback marks a significant shift after weeks of relatively stable pricing, signaling renewed pressure on the broader energy complex. Both WTI and Brent benchmarks reflected weakness, though trading volumes and technical levels will determine whether the decline finds support.

Demand concerns have resurfaced as a primary headwind for crude prices. Economic data from major consuming regions, including signs of slower manufacturing activity, has prompted traders to reassess fuel consumption outlooks. Additionally, reports of rising crude inventories in key markets have added weight to bearish sentiment, offsetting supply-side tightness that prevailed in earlier weeks.

Geopolitical risks that supported prices in recent months appear to have temporarily receded from immediate market focus. While longer-term supply uncertainty remains relevant—particularly regarding OPEC+ production management and tensions in key shipping lanes—near-term trading has shifted toward macro fundamentals and inventory dynamics. The repricing underscores how quickly sentiment can shift when demand signals weaken.

Traders and analysts will be watching for confirmation of whether this dip represents a correction within a broader uptrend or the start of a more sustained decline. Support levels and production decision timelines from OPEC+ members will be critical watch points in the sessions ahead. Energy markets remain sensitive to both demand revisions and any unexpected supply disruptions, making volatility a fixture of current price action.
