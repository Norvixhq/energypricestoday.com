Oil price predicted to remain above $100 for rest of year

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Energy analysts broadly expect crude to sustain triple-digit pricing through year’s end, citing persistent supply constraints and steady global demand. The consensus reflects ongoing tensions in key production regions and OPEC+’s measured approach to output policy. While near-term volatility remains inevitable, the structural backdrop appears supportive of floors near $100 per barrel.

Supply-side pressures continue to underpin the market. Geopolitical risks in the Middle East, sanctions-driven disruptions, and underinvestment in new production capacity all limit near-term barrels available to global refiners. Maintenance schedules and unplanned outages in mature fields further tighten the balance, leaving little room for error.

Demand dynamics, particularly from Asia, have remained resilient despite economic headwinds in developed economies. China’s post-COVID recovery and India’s growth trajectory continue to absorb crude imports at meaningful levels. This foundational demand support reduces the risk of a sharp downdraft in pricing.

Downside scenarios would require either a significant supply boost or an unexpected demand shock—both of which analysts view as less probable in the near term. OPEC+ guidance and the absence of major new production startups suggest the market will remain relatively tight. Market participants are advised to monitor geopolitical developments and economic data closely, as either could shift the outlook.
