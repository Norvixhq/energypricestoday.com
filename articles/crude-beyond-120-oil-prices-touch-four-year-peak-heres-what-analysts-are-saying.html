Crude beyond $120: Oil prices touch four-year peak, here's what analysts are saying

Staff May 4, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks have reached their highest levels since 2018, signaling a sharp tightening in global energy markets. The rally reflects a confluence of supply constraints, geopolitical tensions, and persistent demand resilience across key consuming regions. Analysts remain divided on whether current prices are sustainable or vulnerable to demand-side corrections.

Supply-side pressures continue to dominate the narrative. OPEC+ production cuts, underperforming non-OPEC output, and unplanned disruptions in critical chokepoints have compressed available barrels. Meanwhile, refinery outages and seasonal maintenance schedules have amplified price support at the crude level.

On the demand front, economic data from the U.S. and Asia has painted a mixed picture. While some indicators suggest softening growth, transportation and industrial fuel consumption remain relatively steady, preventing the kind of demand destruction that typically caps rallies. Energy traders remain cautious about recession signals that could reverse recent gains.

Market participants are closely monitoring geopolitical developments and potential policy shifts that could ease supply bottlenecks. Any announcement of additional OPEC+ production increases or a resolution to key regional tensions could trigger meaningful downside volatility. For now, the balance sheet remains tight, supporting the bull case despite headwinds from elevated prices.
