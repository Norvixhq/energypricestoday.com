Gas prices rise to $4.46 per gallon and oil jumps as shippers stay out of Strait of Hormuz

Staff May 5, 2026 5 min read

The U.S. average retail gasoline price has climbed to $4.46 per gallon amid a broader rally in crude oil markets. The increase reflects growing concerns over supply disruptions tied to shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world’s most critical petroleum chokepoints. Refiners and traders are closely monitoring the situation as it poses direct implications for fuel availability and pricing across North America.

The Strait of Hormuz, nestled between Iran and Oman, handles roughly one-third of global seaborne oil trade. When shippers avoid the waterway due to security risks or geopolitical tensions, crude typically must take longer alternate routes, raising transport costs and tightening near-term supply. Recent reports indicate reduced traffic through the passage, creating upward pressure on both WTI and Brent crude benchmarks.

Oil markets have responded sharply to the supply concerns, with crude prices moving higher as traders price in potential supply losses. Shipping delays and reroutings increase delivered costs to U.S. refineries, which in turn flow through to the pump. The combination of tighter near-term crude availability and elevated logistics expenses is translating directly into higher gasoline costs for consumers.

Market participants are watching for any resolution to the Strait of Hormuz concerns that could ease pressure on crude and gasoline prices. Meantime, refiners are adjusting their purchasing and scheduling to accommodate longer shipping routes. The situation underscores how geopolitical events in key chokepoints remain a primary driver of U.S. energy prices and consumer costs.
