Oil prices today: Crude jumps after Trump rejects Iran’s latest offer; Strait of Hormuz remains shut

Staff May 12, 2026 5 min read

WTI and Brent crude advanced on geopolitical risk premium following the U.S. administration’s rejection of Iran’s latest diplomatic overture. The move signals minimal near-term progress in nuclear negotiations, reducing investor confidence in a near-term easing of sanctions on Iranian crude exports. Market participants are pricing in sustained supply-side tightness as a result.

The Strait of Hormuz remains functionally closed to normal traffic, with shipping routes disrupted amid regional tensions. Roughly 20 percent of global seaborne crude oil typically transits the chokepoint; any prolonged blockade or heightened risk of disruption feeds directly into crude valuations. Insurance and routing premiums for tankers in the region have remained elevated.

Upstream producers and traders are monitoring the situation closely, as additional supply disruptions could tighten global markets further. OPEC+ compliance and production decisions will likely be viewed through the lens of geopolitical supply risk in the coming weeks. Refined product markets are also feeling secondary effects from transport uncertainty.

The energy sector will continue to track diplomatic developments and any shifts in U.S. policy toward Iran. Until a clear path to sanctions relief emerges—or is definitively ruled out—crude markets are likely to remain supported by geopolitical risk. Market participants should watch for any statements from the State Department or signals on international negotiations.
