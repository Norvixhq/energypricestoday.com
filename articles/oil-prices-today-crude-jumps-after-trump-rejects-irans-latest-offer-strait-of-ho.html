Oil prices today: Crude jumps after Trump rejects Iran’s latest offer; Strait of Hormuz remains shut

Staff May 11, 2026 5 min read

West Texas Intermediate and Brent crude rose sharply in early trading following the Trump administration’s rejection of Iran’s latest diplomatic proposal. The move signals a hardening stance on Tehran and reduces near-term prospects for sanctions relief that might unlock additional Iranian barrels. Markets have grown accustomed to elevated geopolitical risk premiums, and today’s price action reflects renewed uncertainty around potential escalation.

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to normal traffic, a critical chokepoint through which roughly one-fifth of global seaborne oil flows under normal conditions. Shipping delays and rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope add days to voyage times and increase freight costs. This supply constraint has become a structural feature of current market dynamics rather than a temporary disruption.

Energy traders are weighing competing forces: slowing global demand signals from manufacturing data and interest rate expectations versus supply tightness from Iran sanctions and Red Sea shipping disruptions. The price reaction today underscores how geopolitical developments can override broader macroeconomic headwinds, at least in the short term. Refiners and major consumers are monitoring the situation closely as winter demand typically strengthens.

Going forward, market participants will watch for any signs of direct U.S.–Iran negotiations or further escalation that could alter the supply outlook. The current impasse means Iranian crude remains off the global market, supporting prices for competing producers including OPEC+ members and U.S. shale operators. Any breakthrough—or breakdown—in diplomatic channels could trigger significant volatility.
