SC gas prices fluctuate as fight over Straight of Hormuz continues

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

South Carolina’s retail gasoline market is experiencing volatility as tensions in the Strait of Hormuz persist, a critical chokepoint through which roughly one-fifth of global crude shipments flow. Supply concerns tied to regional geopolitical friction have kept downstream fuel costs in flux, rippling through state pump prices week to week. Retailers and consumers across SC are navigating uncertainty that extends beyond typical seasonal patterns.

The Strait of Hormuz dispute underscores how concentrated global oil transit points influence even regional fuel markets thousands of miles away. Any disruption to tanker traffic or production in the Middle East can tighten crude supplies for U.S. refiners, ultimately affecting what motorists pay at the pump. South Carolina’s position as both a consumer and refining hub makes it particularly sensitive to these international developments.

Wholesale rack prices and refinery utilization rates have fluctuated in response to headlines from the region, creating uneven pricing across SC service stations. Independent retailers and major chains have adjusted margins and fuel acquisition strategies to hedge against further volatility. Drivers should expect continued movement in daily prices rather than the stability typically seen outside periods of geopolitical tension.

Energy markets will likely remain focused on Hormuz developments in the coming weeks, with any escalation or de-escalation capable of shifting crude futures and, in turn, South Carolina’s gasoline landscape. Traders, refiners, and consumers are all monitoring shipping reports and regional news closely. Until the situation stabilizes, SC motorists can expect the current price swings to persist.
