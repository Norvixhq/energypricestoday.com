Oil prices hit new wartime high before easing, as gas prices expected to go up

Staff Apr 30, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil reached its highest level since the onset of major global conflict this week, driven by geopolitical tensions and supply concerns that have rattled energy markets. The spike reflects persistent anxiety among traders over potential disruptions to crude flows and refining capacity. WTI and Brent both surged as investors reassessed risk premiums in the face of escalating regional instability.

The rally has since moderated from peak levels, though crude remains elevated relative to pre-conflict baselines. Analysts attribute the pullback to profit-taking and short-term technical adjustments rather than fundamental shifts in supply or demand. Market participants are watching for fresh data on production cuts and inventory levels that could influence the next leg of trading.

Retail gasoline prices are expected to climb in response to the crude surge, with pump prices already reflecting upstream cost pressures across major markets. Refiners are passing through higher feedstock costs, and margins have widened as processing demand recovers. Motorists should anticipate further increases at the pump over the coming days to weeks depending on crude’s trajectory.

The disconnect between crude’s record spikes and actual supply losses underscores market nervousness rather than current shortages. Storage levels remain adequate in many regions, but hedging activity and positioning shifts are amplifying price swings. Energy traders continue to monitor shipping routes, OPEC+ policy, and geopolitical headlines for signals that could trigger additional volatility.
