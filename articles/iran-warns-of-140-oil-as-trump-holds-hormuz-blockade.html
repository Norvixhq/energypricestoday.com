Iran Warns of $140 Oil as Trump Holds Hormuz Blockade

Staff May 3, 2026 5 min read

Iran’s government has reiterated threats to block the Strait of Hormuz in response to continued U.S. pressure under the incoming Trump administration. The warning comes as tensions escalate over sanctions, nuclear negotiations, and regional military posturing. Officials have suggested that such a blockade could send crude prices sharply higher, though the exact figure cited remains contested across diplomatic channels.

The Strait of Hormuz remains one of the world’s most critical chokepoints, with roughly one-third of global seaborne oil passing through its waters daily. Any sustained closure would immediately disrupt supply flows to Asia, Europe, and beyond, forcing markets to price in significant supply loss and drawing on strategic reserves. Traders have factored geopolitical risk premiums into crude benchmarks for months, but a prolonged blockade would test those assumptions severely.

The Trump administration’s stated approach to Iran policy differs from its predecessor, with a focus on maximum pressure and deterrence rather than negotiated constraints. U.S. military presence in the region, including naval deployments and air assets, underscores Washington’s commitment to keeping the waterway open. The combination of Iranian threats and American resolve has created an uncertain standoff with clear implications for global energy security.

Energy markets are watching this dynamic closely, as WTI and Brent crude remain sensitive to any Hormuz-related supply shock. Refiners and traders have begun stress-testing their operations against extended disruption scenarios. Until diplomatic signals shift or military posturing eases, the risk premium embedded in oil prices is likely to persist and potentially widen.
