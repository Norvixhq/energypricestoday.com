Crude oil surges 8% in a week to near $110 as Iran war tensions simmer again. Where are prices headed?

Staff May 16, 2026 5 min read

West Texas Intermediate crude has climbed roughly 8% over the past week, driven primarily by renewed geopolitical concerns in the Middle East. Tensions between Iran and Israel have reignited after a period of relative calm, prompting traders to reassess supply risk premiums. The rally has pushed WTI toward the $110 per barrel level, a threshold last seen during similar flare-ups in regional conflict.

The Strait of Hormuz remains the critical focal point for crude markets, with roughly one-third of seaborne oil passing through the waterway daily. Any escalation involving Iran—whether direct action or retaliatory measures—could disrupt this vital shipping lane and tighten global supply. Markets are pricing in this tail risk, though actual supply disruptions have not yet materialized.

On the supply side, OPEC+ production levels and compliance rates continue to anchor expectations. Recent compliance with agreed output cuts has helped support prices, though weak global demand signals from manufacturing data and economic slowdowns elsewhere temper bullish sentiment. The tension between geopolitical upside and demand weakness creates an uncertain intermediate outlook.

Where prices move from here will depend on two variables: the trajectory of Iran tensions and whether economic data confirms sustained demand weakness. A de-escalation or diplomatic breakthrough could erase much of the week’s gains, while further military activity could push prices higher. Traders are also watching U.S. crude inventories and refining utilization rates for clues on underlying demand strength.
