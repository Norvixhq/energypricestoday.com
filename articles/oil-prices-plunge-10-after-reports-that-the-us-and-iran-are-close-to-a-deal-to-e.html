Oil prices plunge 10% after reports that the US and Iran are close to a deal to end the war

Staff May 6, 2026 5 min read

Crude markets sold off sharply following reports that diplomatic negotiations between the United States and Iran have accelerated, with both sides reportedly nearing agreement on terms to de-escalate regional tensions. The sharp decline reflects investor concerns that a resolution could ease supply concerns that have underpinned oil valuations since tensions in the Middle East intensified. Traders have already begun pricing in the possibility of reduced geopolitical premium if hostilities diminish.

A lasting ceasefire or normalization agreement could have significant implications for global oil supply, particularly given Iran’s position as a major crude producer. Any lifting of sanctions or removal of trade barriers could potentially allow Iranian crude back into international markets at scale, adding barrels to a market already grappling with elevated production from other sources. The prospect alone has been sufficient to trigger selling pressure across both WTI and Brent benchmarks.

Energy analysts note that the magnitude of any supply response would depend heavily on the specific terms of any agreement and the timeline for implementation. Current production levels, refinery capacity, and existing inventory builds all factor into how meaningful additional Iranian barrels would be to global balances. Markets are likely to remain volatile as details of negotiations emerge and as traders assess the probability and scope of any deal.

For now, the sharp selloff underscores how geopolitical risk premium can evaporate quickly when peace becomes a credible possibility. Downstream sectors and consumers have long sought relief from elevated energy costs, and any agreement reducing Middle East tensions could translate into lower pump prices in the months ahead. Energy traders will be watching diplomatic developments closely as the market reprices its risk assessments.
