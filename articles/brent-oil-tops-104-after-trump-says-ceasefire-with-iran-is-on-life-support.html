Brent oil tops $104 after Trump says ceasefire with Iran is on 'life support'

Staff May 12, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil markets rallied on Tuesday as geopolitical tensions in the Middle East intensified following comments from former President Donald Trump regarding U.S.–Iran relations. Brent crude jumped above $104 per barrel, driven by concerns that a potential breakdown in diplomatic efforts could destabilize one of the world’s most critical energy-producing regions. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global oil passes daily, remains a focal point for supply risk whenever U.S.–Iran tensions escalate.

Trump’s characterization of ceasefire negotiations as being on “life support” suggests diminishing hopes for de-escalation in the near term. Market participants have grown accustomed to pricing in elevated geopolitical risk premiums whenever dialogue between Washington and Tehran falters. Even unconfirmed rhetoric about U.S.–Iran relations can move oil prices, as traders weigh the tail risk of supply disruptions or direct military action.

Energy markets have shown heightened sensitivity to Middle East headlines in recent months, with crude particularly responsive to any signal of escalating conflict. A sustained move above $104 Brent signals that investors see material downside protection in oil prices given current geopolitical uncertainty. Refiners and energy consumers are monitoring developments closely, as further price rallies could ripple through global energy costs.

Traders will watch for additional statements from U.S. officials and any response from Iranian leadership in the coming days. The energy market’s reaction underscores how political developments in the Middle East can override broader macroeconomic signals. Until diplomatic conditions stabilize or show clearer signs of progress, oil markets are likely to remain supported by geopolitical risk premium.
