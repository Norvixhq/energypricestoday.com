Brent oil tops $104 after Trump says ceasefire with Iran is on 'life support'

Staff May 11, 2026 5 min read

Crude prices moved higher Wednesday as geopolitical tensions in the Middle East resurfaced following comments from former President Donald Trump regarding diplomatic efforts with Iran. Brent crude climbed past $104 per barrel, reflecting investor concerns that a potential breakdown in negotiations could escalate regional risk. The uptick underscores how sensitive oil markets remain to any shift in U.S.–Iran relations, particularly given Tehran’s influence over regional stability and shipping lanes.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint for global crude flows, with roughly one-fifth of the world’s seaborne oil passing through its waters. Any escalation in U.S.–Iran tensions historically triggers a risk premium on crude, as markets fear potential supply disruptions or military action. Traders have been monitoring diplomatic developments closely, and Trump’s comments suggesting deteriorating negotiations pushed sentiment toward caution.

Energy markets have oscillated this year between demand concerns and geopolitical risk premiums. The move above $104 on Brent signals that risk perceptions are temporarily outweighing recession fears and recent demand softness. However, global crude inventories remain relatively healthy, which may limit how far prices can rally on political headlines alone.

Market participants will likely keep close watch on official statements from U.S. and Iranian officials in coming days, as well as any changes to sanctions policy or military posturing. The current oil price environment reflects a delicate balance between structural demand headwinds and supply-side risks. Near-term crude direction may hinge on whether diplomatic developments stabilize or continue to deteriorate.
