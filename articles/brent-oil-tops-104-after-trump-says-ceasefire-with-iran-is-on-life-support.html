Brent oil tops $104 after Trump says ceasefire with Iran is on 'life support'

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Brent crude climbed above $104 per barrel on Wednesday following remarks from President Trump suggesting that diplomatic efforts between Washington and Tehran have deteriorated significantly. The geopolitical premium reflects growing concern among traders that escalating U.S.–Iran tensions could disrupt crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil supply. Oil markets have historically responded sharply to any threat of conflict in the Persian Gulf region, where roughly one-third of seaborne traded oil passes daily.

Trump’s characterization of ceasefire negotiations as being on “life support” signals a potential shift in U.S. policy toward Iran after months of indirect diplomatic channels. Energy traders have priced in heightened geopolitical risk, pushing both Brent and WTI crude upward in recent sessions. The language suggests limited runway for further negotiations without a material change in either side’s position.

Market participants are watching for any signals of Iranian retaliation or U.S. military action that could tighten crude supplies or disrupt shipping lanes. Previous confrontations between the two nations have prompted sharp, temporary spikes in oil prices as markets price in supply-shock scenarios. Current inventory levels in OECD countries and production trends from OPEC+ members will influence how sustained any price premium becomes.

Analysts caution that oil prices remain volatile amid competing signals from global demand, monetary policy, and geopolitical risk. While the Iran headline is supporting prices today, longer-term crude direction will depend on whether escalation actually disrupts physical supply or whether diplomatic channels reopen. Energy traders are advised to monitor official statements and shipping traffic data for concrete developments beyond rhetoric.
