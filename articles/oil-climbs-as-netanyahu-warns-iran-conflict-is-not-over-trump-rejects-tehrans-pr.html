Oil climbs as Netanyahu warns Iran conflict is ‘not over,’ Trump rejects Tehran's proposal to end war

Staff May 11, 2026 5 min read

Crude oil prices rose on renewed Middle East tensions following Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s statement that military operations against Iran remain active. The rhetoric signals that diplomatic resolution of the conflict remains distant, sustaining the risk premium that has supported oil markets. Geopolitical supply disruptions in the region have historically driven crude volatility, and investors are reassessing the duration of this conflict.

President Trump’s rejection of Iran’s proposal to de-escalate the conflict underscores the complexity of reaching a negotiated settlement. The U.S. position suggests that diplomatic off-ramps may remain limited in the near term, keeping regional tensions elevated. Energy markets have priced in a baseline level of supply risk given the Strait of Hormuz’s critical role in global oil transit.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a focal point for market participants, given that a significant portion of global crude exports transit the waterway. Any disruption to shipping or infrastructure in the Persian Gulf could constrain supply and support prices. Market participants are monitoring developments closely, though current flows remain largely intact.

Oil markets have shown resilience to geopolitical headlines in recent weeks, with traders balancing supply concerns against demand weakness and OPEC+ production management. The ongoing standoff between Israel and Iran adds another layer of uncertainty to 2024 energy outlooks. Crude’s reaction will likely depend on whether rhetoric escalates to direct action that threatens regional infrastructure or shipping.
