Oil prices today: Crude falls below $100 as Trump announces two-week ceasefire with Iran

Staff May 8, 2026 5 min read

West Texas Intermediate crude dropped below the $100-per-barrel threshold on news of a temporary diplomatic agreement between Washington and Tehran. The two-week ceasefire announcement eased concerns about potential Iranian retaliation for recent regional tensions, which had kept geopolitical risk premiums elevated in energy markets. Markets typically respond to reduced conflict risk with lower crude valuations, as the threat of supply disruptions diminishes.

The ceasefire removes near-term uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint through which roughly one-fifth of global oil passes. Any escalation in U.S.-Iran hostilities has historically triggered sharp rallies in crude as traders price in disruption risk. With diplomatic talks now scheduled, investors are reassessing their hedges and trimming long positions that had priced in geopolitical risk.

Broader market dynamics also weighed on prices, with demand concerns and inventory builds in key markets offsetting the geopolitical relief. The energy sector will continue monitoring the ceasefire’s trajectory closely, as any breakdown in negotiations could quickly reverse today’s losses. Analysts note that the two-week timeline is relatively short, leaving open questions about longer-term resolution.

Traders will watch for official statements from both governments and any indications of whether the ceasefire might extend beyond the initial period. Oil markets remain sensitive to headline risk from the region, and the current pullback may prove temporary if tensions reignite. Energy intelligence providers will track OPEC+ responses and production decisions as additional factors that could influence the price floor in coming sessions.
