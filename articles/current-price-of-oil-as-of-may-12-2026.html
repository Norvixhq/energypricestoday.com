Current price of oil as of May 12, 2026

Staff May 13, 2026 5 min read

Global crude markets on May 12, 2026 reflect ongoing tensions between supply management and demand signals across major trading hubs. Both WTI and Brent crude benchmarks are actively traded in response to geopolitical developments, OPEC+ production decisions, and macroeconomic conditions affecting fuel consumption worldwide. Market participants continue to monitor refinery utilization rates and inventory levels as key drivers of near-term price direction.

OPEC+ production policy remains a central anchor for market expectations. The cartel’s most recent output adjustments and member compliance rates directly influence the global supply balance and set the tone for price discovery across all crude grades. Traders are weighing the sustainability of current production levels against seasonal demand patterns heading into the summer driving season in the Northern Hemisphere.

Upstream activity indicators, including rig counts in shale-producing regions and offshore platforms, continue to shape longer-term supply outlooks. Geopolitical risks in strategic chokepoints, particularly the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea shipping corridor, remain priced into market risk premiums. Any disruption to these critical transit routes would quickly reprice crude contracts across all expiries.

Downstream pressures from refinery maintenance schedules and seasonal crack spreads are influencing crude demand at current price levels. LNG markets and natural gas prices also play an indirect role in crude valuation through switching economics and global energy cost benchmarking. Market participants should monitor official inventory reports and production data releases for confirmation of supply–demand balance shifts in coming sessions.
