Oil prices fall below $100 as U.S. waits for Iran response to framework deal

Staff May 7, 2026 5 min read

Crude benchmarks retreated from the three-digit threshold this week as market participants adopted a cautious stance ahead of Tehran’s formal response to an emerging nuclear framework agreement. The pullback reflects broader uncertainty about how negotiations will affect Iranian crude exports and broader Middle East stability. WTI and Brent have been volatile throughout the ongoing talks, swinging on headlines about sanctions relief and production capacity.

A potential agreement on Iran’s nuclear program could eventually lead to the lifting of oil export restrictions, introducing significant supply back into global markets. Tehran has repeatedly stated its readiness to boost exports once sanctions are lifted, and traders are pricing in the possibility of incremental barrels entering the market over coming months. The scale and timing of that return remain key variables that will influence price direction.

Geopolitical risk premiums have moderated as the diplomatic pathway has clarified, though tensions in the Strait of Hormuz and broader Middle East dynamics continue to warrant attention. Even modest progress in nuclear talks has historically reduced the perceived risk of supply disruptions in the region. Market participants are now focused on the mechanics and timeline of any final agreement rather than binary escalation scenarios.

Energy traders are monitoring Iran’s official response closely, as it will provide clearer signals about the probability and sequencing of sanctions relief. Market pricing will likely remain range-bound until a more definitive statement emerges from Tehran or other major parties to the talks. The relationship between negotiation progress and crude supply expectations will remain central to price formation in the coming days.
